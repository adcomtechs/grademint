Getting Started

What Is GPA?

Grade Point Average — the single number that summarises your academic performance.

Your Grade Point Average (GPA) is a weighted average that condenses all your course grades into a single number. Rather than listing twenty individual scores, universities use GPA to communicate academic standing quickly — to the registrar, scholarship committees, and employers.

💡
Key insight: GPA is weighted, not simple. A 3-unit course contributes three times as much to your GPA as a 1-unit course. This is why your score in MTH 201 (3 units) matters more than GST 101 (1 unit).

At Nigerian universities the GPA is calculated on a 5.0 scale. Each letter grade (A through F) maps to a grade point value, and those points are weighted by credit units before being averaged.

Why GPA Matters

  • Scholarships — most merit awards have a minimum CGPA requirement (typically 3.50+).
  • Postgraduate admission — M.Sc. and MBA programmes often require a 2nd Class Upper (≥ 3.50) or better.
  • Employment — many Nigerian graduate schemes filter applications by CGPA (common cutoff: 3.00).
  • Departmental standing — academic probation is often triggered when GPA drops below a threshold.
  • Honours degree class — your programme-exit classification (First Class, 2nd Upper, etc.) is entirely GPA-derived.

What Is CGPA?

Cumulative GPA — the running average across every semester of your programme.

While GPA measures performance within a single semester, CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) measures performance across your entire academic career so far. Every course you have ever registered — passed or failed — feeds into your CGPA.

⚠️
A failed course drags your CGPA for the rest of your programme. Even if you pass the supplementary exam, the original F grade and its zero quality points remain in the CGPA denominator at most Nigerian universities. Check your institution's carry-over policy carefully.

GPA vs CGPA — side by side

GPA CGPA
Scope One semester All semesters to date
Reset each semester? Yes — starts fresh No — carries forward
Used for classification? No Yes (exit CGPA)
Can it jump quickly? Yes Gradually — inertia increases each semester

Because CGPA accumulates over time, early semesters carry disproportionate weight. A poor 100L performance is much harder to recover from in 400L than the same poor performance in 300L — because by 300L you have more future credit units available to dilute it.

The GPA Formula — Demystified

The exact arithmetic behind every GPA calculation.

The GPA formula is a weighted arithmetic mean. It weights each course's grade point by the number of credit units the course carries.

Semester GPA GPA = Σ (Grade Point × Credit Units) Σ (Credit Units)
CGPA CGPA = Σ (All Quality Points across all semesters) Σ (All Credit Units across all semesters)

Worked Example

Suppose you registered these courses in your 100L First Semester:

Course Score Grade Grade Point Credit Units Quality Points
MTH 101 78 A 5.0 3 15.0
CSC 101 63 B 4.0 3 12.0
ENG 101 55 C 3.0 2 6.0
GST 101 72 A 5.0 1 5.0
PHY 101 47 D 2.0 3 6.0
Totals 12 44.0
Semester GPA = 44.0 ÷ 12 = 3.67
✓
This is exactly what GPA Pro computes when you enter those five courses. Try it — the result will match to two decimal places.

Quality Points Explained

The fundamental unit of every GPA calculation.

A quality point is simply a course's grade point multiplied by its credit units. It is the "contribution" of one course to your overall GPA.

📐
Quality Points = Grade Point × Credit Units
A grade of B (4.0) in a 3-unit course contributes 4.0 × 3 = 12.0 quality points. The same B in a 1-unit course contributes only 4.0 × 1 = 4.0 quality points.

This is why high-unit courses are both an opportunity and a risk: an A in a 4-unit course adds 20 quality points — more than two A grades in two 2-unit courses would add separately.

⚠️
Note on institutional variation: A small number of universities use slightly different cutoffs (e.g., 75+ for A, 65–74 for B). Always verify your institution's specific scale in your student handbook. GPA Pro uses the NUC standard scale above.

Why the jump from E to D is the most important boundary

Notice that grades E (40–44) and D (45–49) are both passing grades but earn only 1.0 and 2.0 points respectively. The jump from an F to an E is worth 1 quality point per credit unit. But the jump from E (1.0) to C (3.0) — just 6–10 more marks on your exam — is worth 2 extra quality points per unit. This is the most cost-effective improvement a struggling student can make.

Honor Degree Classifications

What your exit CGPA means for the class of degree you receive.

At the end of your programme, your final CGPA determines the class of degree printed on your certificate and transcript. This is a permanent classification that follows you throughout your career.

🏅
First Class Honours
CGPA: 4.50 – 5.00

The highest degree classification. Typically required for direct PhD admission and most overseas funded postgraduate programmes. Fewer than 5% of graduates achieve this in most programmes.

🥇
Second Class Honours (Upper)
CGPA: 3.50 – 4.49

Widely regarded as the minimum for competitive employment, most scholarships, and postgraduate admissions. The most common target classification.

🥈
Second Class Honours (Lower)
CGPA: 2.40 – 3.49

A valid degree classification. Some employers and institutions accept this with strong other credentials. Most Nigerian NYSC placements do not discriminate between Upper and Lower 2nd Class.

🥉
Third Class Honours
CGPA: 1.50 – 2.39

A degree is still conferred. Many graduate programmes and employers have minimum CGPA requirements that this class does not meet, but it opens pathways for professional certifications and experience-based careers.

📋
Pass
CGPA: 1.00 – 1.49

The student graduates but without an Honours classification. Policies vary — some universities issue an Ordinary degree in this range.

Pass, Fail & Carry-over Courses

What an F actually means — and how it follows your CGPA.

In Nigeria's university system, an F (score below 40) means the course is failed and must be repeated (carried over to the next session). Crucially, the F grade is not erased when you retake the course — both the original F and the new grade appear on your transcript and are used to compute your CGPA.

Concrete impact of one F on your CGPA

Suppose you score 0 quality points on a 3-unit course (F grade):

Without the F 3.80 CGPA 60 CU, 228 QP
→
With one F (3 CU) 3.62 CGPA 63 CU, 228 QP (0 added)

A single F in a 3-unit course dropped this student from a potential First Class corridor into the upper range of a 2nd Class Upper. They would need to earn additional quality points in future semesters just to recover ground.

📌
Supplementary exams: Many universities allow students to sit a supplementary exam for a failed course. The maximum score achievable on a supplementary is often capped at 40–50 (grade E or D). Check your senate regulations — passing a supplementary does not erase the original F in CGPA computation at most institutions.

The What-If Calculator

Find out exactly what GPA you need in future semesters to hit your target.

The What-If calculator answers the question every student asks: "What GPA do I need to graduate with a First Class?"

How it works

Enter your target CGPA and the number of credit units you plan to take before graduation. The calculator solves this equation:

Required GPA = (Target CGPA × (Current CU + Planned CU) − Current Quality Points) ÷ Planned CU

Practical example

You currently have a 3.40 CGPA over 60 credit units. You want to graduate with a 3.50 (2nd Class Upper). You have 60 credit units remaining.

Current CGPA 3.40 60 CU earned
→
Required GPA (60 CU) 3.60 Per remaining semester avg

A 3.60 per semester average is ambitious but achievable. The tool will tell you if your target is mathematically impossible given the remaining credit units (e.g. you'd need a 6.2/5.0 — which is impossible).

How to Raise Your GPA

Evidence-based strategies, not generic advice.

Raising a CGPA is harder than people expect because of inertia — the more credits you have accumulated, the harder each new semester can move the needle. Here are strategies that actually work mathematically.

1. Attack the unit-heavy courses first

A 4-unit course is worth four times as much as a 1-unit course. Plot your remaining courses by credit unit and focus revision energy on the heaviest ones. Raising a 4-unit course from a C (3.0 × 4 = 12 QP) to an A (5.0 × 4 = 20 QP) gains you 8 quality points — the same as getting A instead of C in eight separate 1-unit courses.

2. Prioritise the E→C boundary

Moving a score from 40 (E, 1.0 GP) to 50 (C, 3.0 GP) adds 2 quality points per credit unit. Moving from a 60 (B, 4.0 GP) to 70 (A, 5.0 GP) adds only 1. If you're scoring in the E/D range in multiple courses, fixing those has a larger payoff than polishing already-passing grades.

3. Never fail a high-unit course

An F in a 3-unit course is equivalent to zero quality points across 3 units. If you pass at E (1.0), you at least earn 3 quality points. The difference over a full programme can be 20+ quality points — the equivalent of converting several B grades to A grades.

4. Use the What-If calculator before each semester

Before registration, calculate the GPA you need next semester to stay on track for your target classification. This gives you a concrete numerical goal rather than a vague aspiration.

⏰
The earlier, the better. Raising your CGPA from 3.40 to 3.50 requires a 3.60 average over the remaining 60 units. The same increase over only 30 remaining units requires a 3.80. Time really does run out.

Credit Units & Their Weight

Not all courses are equal — here's how to think about your course load.

Credit units (sometimes called "credit hours") represent the workload and weight of a course. In Nigeria, 1 credit unit typically corresponds to 1 lecture hour per week for a 15-week semester — plus practical, tutorial, or lab hours.

Typical credit unit assignments

  • 1 unit — Use of Library (GST 101), PE, short electives
  • 2 units — Most general studies (GST) courses, some science practicals
  • 3 units — Standard core courses (MTH 201, CSC 301, etc.)
  • 4 units — Heavy core technical courses, some final-year courses
  • 6 units — Final-year project (PROJ 401) at many institutions
🎯
Final-year project strategy: If your project is 6 units, an A grade earns you 30 quality points — more than any other single course in your programme. Treat it accordingly.

The First Class Roadmap

What it mathematically takes to graduate with a 4.50+ CGPA.

First Class (≥ 4.50 CGPA) means that across your entire programme, your average course performance must be between A and B+ — every semester, not just a few good ones. Here is what that looks like numerically.

The minimum grade mix for First Class

To maintain a 4.50 CGPA, you need a weighted average grade point of at least 4.50 per credit unit. That means in any semester with 15 credit units, you need at least 67.5 quality points. Some possible grade combinations:

  • All 5 A grades (5.0 × 15 = 75 QP) — exceeds target ✓
  • 10 units of A + 5 units of B: (50 + 20 = 70 QP) ÷ 15 = 4.67 ✓
  • 9 units of A + 6 units of B: (45 + 24 = 69 QP) ÷ 15 = 4.60 ✓
  • 6 units of A + 9 units of B: (30 + 36 = 66 QP) ÷ 15 = 4.40 ✗

In plain terms: you can afford some B grades, but even a single C in a 3-unit course requires compensating with A grades in at least 6 more credit units just to break even at 4.50 for that semester.

🏅
First Class is not about perfection — it is about consistency. One outstanding semester cannot save a programme of average ones. But a steady diet of A and B grades in major courses, with no fails, gets most students there over 4–5 years.
FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the most common questions.

Does GPA Pro store my data online? ▶
No. All data is stored exclusively in your browser's IndexedDB — a local database on your device. Nothing is sent to any server. Clearing your browser storage will erase your data, so use the Print → PDF feature to make a permanent copy.
Can I use GPA Pro on mobile? ▶
Yes. GPA Pro is fully responsive. The layout adapts to phone screens and works in any modern mobile browser (Chrome, Safari, Firefox). IndexedDB is supported across all modern browsers including mobile ones.
My university uses a different grading scale — can I still use this? ▶
GPA Pro currently uses the NUC standard Nigerian 5.0 scale (A: 70–100, B: 60–69, C: 50–59, D: 45–49, E: 40–44, F: 0–39). If your institution uses different cutoffs, the grades shown may not match — but you can still use the score field and the quality points calculation will be based on the grade point assigned by GPA Pro's scale. Custom scale support is planned for the premium tier.
What does "Previous Institutional Record" mean? ▶
It lets you include credits and quality points from a previous institution or session that are not already entered as semesters in the app. This is useful for transfer students or those who deferred and have an official CGPA from a prior session to account for. See the Previous Institutional Record guide above.
I passed my carry-over exam. Why does my CGPA still look low? ▶
At most Nigerian universities, when you retake a carry-over course, both the original F grade and the new grade count in your CGPA. The F contributed 0 quality points to your numerator but added credit units to your denominator — pulling your average down permanently. You can model this in GPA Pro by entering the course twice (once with the original failing score and once with the retake score).
How is the trend chart calculated? ▶
The trend chart shows two lines: (1) your individual semester GPA for each semester (dashed blue line), and (2) your running CGPA as it accumulates (solid gold line). The CGPA line is recalculated after each semester by summing all quality points and all credit units up to that point.
What's coming in the premium tier? ▶
The premium tier will introduce structured programme tracking — you set your degree duration (3, 4, or 5 years) and GPA Pro auto-generates the level structure (100L through 500L). Each level will have its own GPA, and the system will compute your programme CGPA and final classification. Additional features include carry-over tracking, PDF export, and a target GPA projector that shows what you need in each remaining semester across your full programme.
